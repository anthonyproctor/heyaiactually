Scam calls, deepfakes, and cloned voices

You can't tell what's real anymore.

You can't always tell by ear. You can tell by process: a family code word, a call back on a number you know, and a rule that gift cards and wire transfers are a stop sign.

AI tools that do things you never approved

What your kids are saying to AI

It's just a homework helper.

free, no signup

Three things to do tonight. Fifteen minutes, one phone call.

Start with the scam call, since it's the quickest one to fix. Do these with your parents, your partner, or your kids.

  1. Pick a family code word.

    Agree on a secret word or phrase, in person or on a call you started, not in a text. Make it something a stranger couldn't guess from your social media. If anyone ever calls sounding like family in trouble, ask for the word first.

    FBI advice, Dec 2024 →

  2. Hang up and call back on a number you know.

    Don't trust the voice, even if it sounds perfect. End the call and phone the person on the number you already have for them. If you can't reach them, try another relative or a friend.

    FTC advice on family emergency scams →

  3. Make certain payments a stop sign.

    Gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, and payment apps are what scammers ask for because the money is hard to get back. Tell your family that anyone who demands one of these, urgently, is a red flag no matter who they sound like.

    FTC: how to avoid a scam →

For your own AI tools: open the settings of any AI app you use and look for what it's allowed to do without asking. Buying, sending, reading your email. Turn off what you didn't mean to allow, and check your card statement for charges you don't recognize.

For your kids: ask them to show you the chatbots they use, and ask like you're curious, not like you're catching them. Common Sense Media recommends that no one under 18 use AI companions.

If you've already been scammed, report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and tell someone you trust. It happens to smart people.

Is the Family AI Safety guide ready?

What is a family code word and why does it help?

It's a secret word or phrase your family agrees on in person. If someone calls sounding like a relative in trouble, you ask for the word. A cloned voice can copy how someone sounds, but it can't know a word only your family knows. The FBI recommends this in its December 2024 public service announcement.
